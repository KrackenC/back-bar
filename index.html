How to Use Back Bar

Tell Back Bar what's on your shelf, and it shows every cocktail you can pour right now, plus the ones you're a bottle away from.

  1. Stock your back bar

    Under Your Back Bar, type what you have: a bottle, a mixer or a juice. Add several at once by separating them with commas, like gin, lime juice, simple syrup. Brand names work too (Cointreau, Campari). You can also browse the category lists below the box and tap to add, or tap Start with the essentials on the welcome panel. Once your shelf has bottles it folds down to one line; tap it to edit.

  2. Keep two bars if you like

    Switch between Home Bar and Cabin Bar at the top of the shelf. Tap the pencil on either one to rename it. Each keeps its own list of bottles.

  3. See what you can make

    What You Can Make lists every recipe. A green Ready to pour tag means you have everything; Missing: names the one thing you still need. The Ready and Almost numbers at the top keep score. Back Bar counts close substitutes, so Cointreau covers triple sec and any whiskey covers a recipe that just says whiskey.

  4. Find the right drink

    Search by name or ingredient, or use the filters: Ready, Almost, Favorites, Made before and Mocktails. Closest to ready shows which drinks need just one more bottle, so you know what to buy next. Under the filters you can narrow by spirit, change the sort order, and switch measures between oz and ml.

  5. Plan your next round

    Tap the ☆ on a card to save a favorite. Tap +, or drag a card onto Up Next, to line up as many as three drinks to make next. New This Week highlights recently added recipes.

  6. Mix, then mark it made

    Tap any drink to open its full recipe: exact measures, the garnish, a note on technique and the bar tools you'll need. Tap it again to fold it away. After you pour, tap Made it?. Back Bar remembers when you last made each drink, and the Made before filter shows your history.

Behind the barTips for better drinks

  • Shake anything with citrus, cream or egg, hard, with plenty of ice, for 10 to 15 seconds. Stir drinks made only of spirits (Martini, Manhattan, Negroni) for about 30 seconds, so they stay silky and clear.
  • Squeeze citrus fresh. Fresh lime and lemon are the biggest upgrade you can make. Bottled juice tastes flat.
  • Measure. A jigger keeps every drink balanced and lets you repeat a good one.
  • Chill the glass with ice water or a few minutes in the freezer before pouring a drink served without ice.
  • For egg white drinks, shake once without ice (a dry shake) to build the foam, then again with ice.
  • Top fizzy drinks (soda, tonic, sparkling wine) last, and stir gently so they keep their bubbles.

Step 4Easy homemade syrups and ice

Simple syrup

1 part sugar to 1 part hot water; stir until clear. Keeps about a month in the fridge. Used in 66 recipes.

Honey syrup

1 part honey to 1 part warm water; stir to blend. Keeps about two weeks. Used in 12 recipes.

Ice

Lots of it, made fresh. Large 2-inch cubes melt slowly for drinks served on a big cube; regular cubes are for shaking.
