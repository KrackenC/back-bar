How to Use Back Bar

Tell Back Bar what's on your shelf, and it shows every cocktail you can pour right now, plus the ones you're a bottle away from.

  1. Stock your back bar

    New here? Tap Start with the essentials on the welcome panel to add 13 basics in one go. Or, under BUILD Your Back Bar, type what you have: a bottle, a mixer or a juice. Suggestions appear as you type, and you can add several at once with commas, like gin, lime juice, simple syrup. Brand names work too (Cointreau, Campari). You can also open the category lists below the box and tap to add.

  2. Edit or switch your shelf

    Once it has bottles, your shelf folds down to one line, BUILD Your Back Bar. Tap it to open it again. Keep a second shelf, like a cabin bar, with + Add another bar; switch between them at the top and tap the pencil to rename one. Each keeps its own bottles. Clear empties the shelf, with an Undo for a few seconds.

  3. See what you can make

    What You Can Make lists every recipe. A green Ready to pour tag means you have everything. Missing: names the one thing you still need, and Missing 3 means three things. The Ready and Almost counts in the header appear once your shelf has bottles. Back Bar counts close substitutes, so Cointreau covers triple sec, and any whiskey covers a recipe that just says whiskey.

  4. Find the right drink

    Search by name or ingredient, or tap a filter: All, Ready, Almost, Favorites, Made before, Just added, Up next or Mocktails. From late November to early January a Holidays chip at the top gathers the Christmas ❄️ and New Year's 🥂 drinks. Tap Refine to narrow by Spirit, change the Sort order, or switch measures between oz and ml. Best next bottle shows which one bottle would unlock the most drinks; tap Add to put it on your shelf. Back Bar remembers the filter you used last.

  5. Open a recipe

    Tap any drink to open its full recipe: exact measures, the garnish, a note on technique and the bar tools you'll need. Tap it again to fold it away.

  6. Plan your next round

    Tap the ☆ on a drink to save it as a favorite. Tap + to line it up to make next. The Up next filter lists your line-up in order and shows how many are in it, and tapping + again takes a drink off. Just added shows the newest batch of recipes; new drinks arrive in a monthly or quarterly release.

  7. Mix, then mark it made

    After you pour, open the drink and tap Made it?. Back Bar remembers how many times you've made each one and when, and the Made before filter shows your history.

New to mixing drinks? Bar essentials, at the top of the page, lists the bottles, tools and glasses worth starting with.

Behind the barTips for better drinks

  • Shake anything with citrus, cream or egg, hard, with plenty of ice, for 10 to 15 seconds. Stir drinks made only of spirits (Martini, Manhattan, Negroni) for about 30 seconds, so they stay silky and clear.
  • Squeeze citrus fresh. Fresh lime and lemon are the biggest upgrade you can make. Bottled juice tastes flat.
  • Measure. A jigger keeps every drink balanced and lets you repeat a good one.
  • Chill the glass with ice water or a few minutes in the freezer before pouring a drink served without ice.
  • For egg white drinks, shake once without ice (a dry shake) to build the foam, then again with ice.
  • Top fizzy drinks (soda, tonic, sparkling wine) last, and stir gently so they keep their bubbles.

Your dataSaved on this device

Your shelves, favorites, Up next and Made history stay in this browser only; nothing is sent anywhere. Reset everything, at the bottom of the page, clears it all. To keep a copy or move your bar to another device, use Back up or move your bar, also at the bottom.

Step 4Easy homemade syrups and ice

Simple syrup

1 part sugar to 1 part hot water; stir until clear. Keeps about a month in the fridge. Used in 66 recipes.

Honey syrup

1 part honey to 1 part warm water; stir to blend. Keeps about two weeks. Used in 12 recipes.

Ice

Lots of it, made fresh. Large 2-inch cubes melt slowly for drinks served on a big cube; regular cubes are for shaking.
